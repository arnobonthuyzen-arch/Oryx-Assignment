Research · Diagnosis

Three barriers stand between the product and the buyer

01

Sustainability isn't the dominant driver

Only 12.5% cite environmental sustainability as a motivator, versus 65% for brand trust. Leading with it alone won't convert buyers.

02

Product information can overwhelm

Oryx wants to educate on quality and minerals, but scientific detail risks overwhelming everyday consumers rather than building authority.

03

Premium risks feeling unapproachable

An authoritative, premium identity must still feel within reach — organic and lighthearted — so it doesn't intimidate everyday buyers.

Root cause: habit, trust and switching cost — buyers need a concrete reason to switch

Audience & Strategy

Segment on behaviour · one goal · three objectives · a positioning

Audience & Strategy · Target audience

We segment on behaviour, not sustainability values

Primary

Value-conscious households

  • Regular, practical buyers — prioritise taste, quality, brand and price
  • Pay a premium when the benefit is clear and justified
  • Respond to health, purity and refillability as value-adds
  • Himalayan/pink buyers are the natural entry point
Secondary

The conscious culinary cook

  • Aligns naturally with the refill and sustainability story
  • Important, but the campaign must not narrow to this eco-first group
  • No correlation found between digital engagement and the wider target
  • NFC is a convenience for everyone, not a filter on who we target
Attitude–behaviour gap: stated environmental values rarely convert to purchase on their own (Munro et al., 2023; Lakhan & Ng, 2025)

The Solution · Concepts

Two linked concepts carry the campaign

Concept 01

Salt Within Reach

More Than Meets the Grind

An NFC tag embedded in the ceramic grinder. One tap of a phone unlocks an uncluttered mobile brand experience that brings origin, scientific transparency and the founder's story within reach.

Concept 02

Refillable Not Replaceable

Refill Not Landfill

Reframes the grinder from a disposable dispenser into a permanent culinary tool — targeting refill abandonment by celebrating the lifespan of the ceramic mechanism and building refilling into daily cooking.

One tells consumers why Oryx is worth choosing · the other keeps them coming back
The Solution · Touchpoints

Three touchpoints carry one simple message

Kalahari origin, purity, a ceramic grinder you buy once — and a refill that costs less than a new grinder.

01

At the shelf

Point-of-sale materials lead with origin, purity and the ceramic mechanism. The refill box sits beside the grinder so “buy one grinder” is visible at the moment of choice.

02

On the grinder

An NFC tag in the grinder opens the Salt Within Reach landing page with a tap — no app, no QR, nothing printed on the label.

03

Online

Educational digital content and social media tell the same story in the same words, backed by a mobile-friendly site where refills are one step away.

Sustainability appears at every touchpoint — always alongside convenience, longevity and cost saving
The Solution · The technology

What an NFC tag actually is

A coin-sized antenna coil and a chip, thinner than a millimetre — fixed under the base of the grinder.

01
No battery
The phone's own field powers the tag — it lasts as long as the grinder
02
No app, no QR code
Hold a phone within a few centimetres and the page opens
03
Nothing printed on the label
The minimalist packaging stays exactly as it is
04
Cents at volume, editable forever
The tag holds a web link, so Oryx can change the destination any time

The Solution · Consumer value

One grinder.
Refilled for life.

If someone has already paid for a good ceramic grinder, there is no reason they should buy a whole new one every time the salt runs out.

Lasts

Ceramic outlasts plastic

A durable mechanism that keeps grinding cleanly — the functional “gold standard” against disposable heads.

Saves

Refills cost less than new grinders

Total cost of ownership beats repeat purchases of plastic grinders — more use from something already paid for.

Bonus

Less waste — “Refill Not Landfill”

Oryx's own framing. The sustainable option is also the practical one — so it supports the message rather than carrying it.

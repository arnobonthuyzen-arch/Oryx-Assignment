The story

One woman, 34 tons of salt, and a dining room table.

Fifteen years ago Samantha Skyring sold her house and bought 34 tons of salt. A single mother, packing bags at her dining room table while caring for her young son. No factory, no investors — just a conviction that the cleanest salt on earth was sitting untouched under a desert.

Today Oryx Desert Salt reaches 23 countries and a team of 35 from communities south of Cape Town. Same salt. Same pan. Same belief.

01 — The source

Three hundred million years in the making

Beneath the pan lies a 55 million ton salt aquifer. Three subterranean streams move through Dwyka rock strata laid down 300 million years ago, replenishing the brine year after year. We spread it across the pan, the African sun does the rest — four weeks at 120°F, and the crystals are ready.

02 — What is inside

Mineral, Not Minimal.

Table salt is stripped of the 80+ elements that make salt worth eating, then topped up with anti-caking agents. Ours is sun-dried and left alone. Nothing added, nothing taken away — a natural source of electrolytes and the trace minerals your body actually runs on.

Gentle, full-bodied flavour that lifts a dish instead of flattening it. The smallest ingredient in the meal, making the biggest difference.

03 — The grinder

Don’t Panic, It’s Ceramic.

Every other grinder head on the shelf is plastic — and every turn shaves a little of it into your food. Ours is ceramic: harder, sharper, and good for 20 refills and more. Oryx spearheaded the ceramic mechanism so the grinder in your hand is a thing you keep, not a thing you finish.

The tag you just touched lives in that same base. Tap it any time — for the story, or to reorder a refill in two taps.
04 — The pan renews itself

Salt for a flavourable tomorrow.

Himalayan salt is blasted out with dynamite and never comes back. Sea salt now carries what we have poured into the ocean. Our aquifer is refilled by the same three streams that have fed it for millennia — we take only what the desert gives back, in step with the rainfall.

Renewable at the source, refillable in your kitchen, and a percentage of every sale going to the ̡Khomani San and Mier communities of the Kalahari.

05 — The name

The animal that cannot live without salt

In July 2000 Samantha walked 75 miles across the Namib over seven days and met the Oryx gazelle face to face. It can go years without water but not weeks without salt, drinking the night dew straight through its hair. Strength and perseverance in the harshest place on earth — that is where the logo on your grinder came from.
